# Behavioral Interview Drills

Behavioral preparation is only real when it is written down and spoken aloud. Each drill names the artifact to produce. Do them with your own stories; the worked solutions use the sample bank from the chapters to show what a strong artifact looks like.

## Foundation: the bank

**D1. Audit a story bank.**
Using the companion's coverage table for the sample bank (chapter 2), *produce:* the signals covered by only one story, the question type most at risk in a real loop, and one concrete story idea to fill each gap for a backend engineer.

**D2. Plan a loop.**
An Amazon loop is expected to ask about Customer Obsession, Ownership, Dive Deep, Have Backbone, Deliver Results, and Earn Trust. *Produce:* a story assignment from the sample bank with no story used twice, noting which assignments are weakest.

**D3. Upgrade a weak answer.**
Weak answer: "We had a lot of incidents with the payment service, so our team decided to refactor it. It took a few months but in the end things got a lot better and everyone was happy." *Produce:* a list of what is missing, and the five questions you would ask the candidate to recover the evidence.

## Structure and delivery

**D4. Rebalance an answer.**
A draft answer has 120 words of situation, 40 of task, 90 of action, and 30 of result. *Produce:* the section shares, the estimated speaking time at 150 words per minute, and a target word budget for each section that meets the chapter 3 targets at the same total length.

**D5. Fix "we".**
Rewrite this action section so that the candidate's own decisions are clear without taking credit for the team's work: "We looked at the logs and we found the problem was in the retry logic. We decided to add idempotency keys and we rolled it out over two weeks."

## Signal-specific stories

**D6. Conflict you lost.**
*Produce:* the six-part outline (stakes, their view, your view, conduct, resolution, aftermath) of a disagreement where the decision went against you and you committed.

**D7. A real failure.**
*Produce:* a five-part failure story (what happened, your part, recovery, prevention, lasting change), and check that the last part contains a later, specific example.

**D8. Mentoring measured by the mentee.**
*Produce:* a mentoring story whose final two sentences describe what the mentee could do afterwards that they could not do before.

## Company formats

**D9. Cover the newest principles.**
*Produce:* one story idea for each of Amazon's two newest Leadership Principles - Strive to be Earth's Best Employer and Success and Scale Bring Broad Responsibility - that a backend engineer at SDE-2 level could plausibly have.

## Challenge

**D10. Full mock behavioral round.**
Have someone ask you six questions drawn from different signals, with at least two probes per answer. Record it. *Produce:* for each answer, its length, its action share, its "I"/"we" balance, and whether each probe found real detail - then list the two stories to rework first.
